Cancún — My Personal Picks

Sun, cenotes, tacos and turquoise water — curated tips for a great trip.

Explore Cancún

About Cancún

Cancún sits on the northeastern tip of Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, where the Caribbean Sea meets powdery white beaches and turquoise lagoons. It's a lively mix of resort beaches, vibrant nightlife, calm family-friendly bays, and easy access to natural wonders and Mayan ruins.

Highlights

  • Beaches: Playa Delfines for the broad shoreline and views, Playa Norte on Isla Mujeres for postcard-perfect water, and smaller hotel zone beaches for convenience.
  • Cenotes & Nature: The Riviera Maya has many cenotes — fresh-water sinkholes great for swimming and snorkeling. Pack water shoes and an underwater camera.
  • Mayan sites: Day trips to Tulum or Chichén Itzá are musts if you love history and impressive ruins.
  • Water activities: Snorkeling, scuba diving (near Isla Mujeres and Isla Contoy), boat tours, paddleboarding, and kiteboarding are all excellent here.
  • Nightlife & Dining: From casual taco stands to high-end seafood restaurants and buzzing bars in the hotel zone, Cancún covers every vibe.

Top Beaches

  • Playa Delfines: Wide public beach with expansive views, great for photos and sunrise walks.
  • Playa Norte (Isla Mujeres): Shallow, calm water and powdery sand — perfect for swimming and relaxing.
  • Playa Tortugas: Easy access, lively vibe, and lots of food stalls nearby — good for families.
  • Playa Chac Mool: Popular with windsurfers and activity rentals; a good mix of local energy and resort access.

Recommended Cenotes

Cenotes are unique sinkholes with crystal-clear fresh water. Each has its own character — some are open-air while others are cavernous and dramatic.

  • Cenote Ik Kil: Near Chichén Itzá, a large and picturesque cenote popular for swimming and photos.
  • Cenote Azul: A family-friendly option with shallow areas and platforms for jumping.
  • Gran Cenote: Near Tulum, famous for snorkeling among stalactites and small fish.
  • Cenote Dos Ojos: Excellent for snorkelers and divers who want long underwater caverns to explore.

Activities & Day Trips

  • Snorkel or dive the Mesoamerican Reef: Book a boat trip to Isla Mujeres or nearby reef sections for colorful marine life.
  • Day trip to Chichén Itzá: Early departures avoid crowds; combine with a cenote swim.
  • Ferry to Isla Mujeres: Rent a golf cart to explore, relax at Playa Norte, or visit Punta Sur.
  • Eco-parks: Xcaret and Xel-Há offer a mix of natural attraction and cultural shows — great for families.
  • Fishing & sunset cruises: Arrange a charter for sport fishing or a relaxed evening sailing trip.

Where to Stay

Hotel Zone offers resort convenience and beach access; downtown (El Centro) has more local restaurants, lower prices, and easy public transit.

Food & Drink

Try fresh ceviche, cochinita pibil, and tacos al pastor. For drinks, local beer and mezcal cocktails pair perfectly with seaside sunsets.

Activities I Recommend

Plan at least one day for an island escape (Isla Mujeres or Isla Contoy), one cenote visit (for a quieter nature experience), and a snorkel trip along the reef. Balance beach downtime with a cultural or adventure day.

  • Sunrise walk on the beach
  • Snorkel the Mesoamerican Reef
  • Visit a cenote early to avoid crowds
  • Evening out in the hotel zone for live music

Practical Tips

  • Best time: November–April for drier weather; shoulder seasons have fewer tourists.
  • Money: Pesos are accepted everywhere; many places accept cards but carry cash for small vendors and tips.
  • Transport: ADO buses and colectivos are affordable; taxis are convenient but confirm the fare.
  • Safety: Stick to well-lit areas at night, keep important documents secure, and use hotel safes when possible.
  • Respect nature: Don't touch coral, and follow local rules around cenotes and protected areas.
